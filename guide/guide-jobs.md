# Picking your pawn's job

"Build a pawn" isn't one path — it's a stack of decisions. Roughly, in order:

1. **Job** — what do you actually want this pawn to be good at?
2. **Class** — some jobs have a dedicated [magic or fighter class](/itemlist/guide/classes/) behind them. Some don't, and that's fine.
3. **Attributes** — Physically Adept and Magically Gifted are the game's own "you're now eligible" traits, gating martial classes ([Gladiator](/itemlist/guide/classes/#gladiator), [Bladedancer](/itemlist/guide/classes/#bladedancer), [Sniper](/itemlist/guide/classes/#sniper)) and magic classes (mages, [Necromancer](/itemlist/guide/classes/#necromancer), [Paladin](/itemlist/guide/classes/#paladin), [Summoner](/itemlist/guide/classes/#summoner), [Druid](/itemlist/guide/classes/#druid)) respectively. Both are buyable on their own, but buying the class trait directly works too — think of the attribute as flavor and insurance, not a hard requirement.
4. **Implants** — bionic and archotech body parts. These stack on top of any class, and they're the whole answer for jobs that don't have one.
5. **Gear** — weapons and armor suited to melee, ranged, or casting.
6. **Enchantments** — once a pawn has any mana pool, gemstone enchanting works on whatever gear they're already carrying. Seven enchantment slots (max mana, cooldown, resistance, regen, force, mana cost, XP gain), and gear crafted from manaweave or demonhide takes enchantments better than normal materials. This layer doesn't care what class you picked — it's universal.

Full detail on every class — bonuses, penalties, restrictions, spells — lives on the **[Classes page](/itemlist/guide/classes/)**. This page is just "what job, what class."

With that stack in mind, here's what actually exists for each job. Jump to one:

## Colony jobs

#### Miner
[Geomancer](/itemlist/guide/classes/#geomancer) is the dedicated pick: +25% mining speed, +25% mining yield, and its master spell can drop a minable meteor on command. [Psionic](/itemlist/guide/classes/#psionic) covers mining too, just as one piece of a much broader labor boost rather than a specialty. [Gladiator](/itemlist/guide/classes/#gladiator) is a real side-pick if you're building a fighter anyway — its Mining Speed and Global Work Speed bonuses stack, landing around +44% actual mining speed despite being a combat class first.
[↑ Back to top](#top)

#### Researcher
[Technomancer](/itemlist/guide/classes/#technomancer): +25% research speed, +20% global learning. This is a case where the class does essentially all the work. [Chronomancer](/itemlist/guide/classes/#chronomancer) actually beats it on paper — +40% research speed, +25% global learning — but it's buying a combat/utility caster for a research bump, not a dedicated research pick the way Technomancer is.
[↑ Back to top](#top)

#### Farmer
Covers Harvester, Planter, and Cutter too. [Druid](/itemlist/guide/classes/#druid) (nature-attuned support caster) and [Chronomancer](/itemlist/guide/classes/#chronomancer) (can flat-out accelerate crop growth) are the two real picks. [Psionic](/itemlist/guide/classes/#psionic)'s general labor boost covers plant work too. A [Necromancer](/itemlist/guide/classes/#necromancer)'s undead can also be set to growing duty, for a very different kind of "farmer."
[↑ Back to top](#top)

#### Crafter
No dedicated class exists for this. Genuine gap. The real answer is implants: bionic or archotech arms and hands stack flat bonuses with zero class prerequisite, zero mana cost, and nothing to manage — just install and forget. This is the job where skipping the class tree entirely and going straight to implants is correct, not a compromise.
[↑ Back to top](#top)

#### Construction
[Golemancer](/itemlist/guide/classes/#golemancer) is the dedicated pick: +20% construction speed, plus +20% smelting speed on top. A [Necromancer](/itemlist/guide/classes/#necromancer)'s undead can also be set to construction work, and Golemancer's own golems can haul materials to support a builder too. For a living pawn who'd rather skip the class tree, bionic arms/hands (Manipulation) work here as well.
[↑ Back to top](#top)

#### Cook
The only class-based cooking bonus in the entire mod belongs to the Lich, which is the [Necromancer](/itemlist/guide/classes/#necromancer)'s late-game evolved form — not something you buy as a starting pick. For an actual cook, implants are the whole strategy here too.
[↑ Back to top](#top)

#### Doctor
[Priest](/itemlist/guide/classes/#priest) is the dedicated pick, and it's not close: +25% surgery success chance, +25% tend quality, +25% tend speed, +25% operation speed. The tradeoff is real too — Priest can't fight at all, and takes pain shock 10% more easily. [Apothecary](/itemlist/guide/classes/#apothecary) offers a smaller secondary medical bump, but it's really a plant/drug specialist first, with real penalties to mining, smelting, and construction.
[↑ Back to top](#top)

#### Morale
[Empath](/itemlist/guide/classes/#empath) (ambient mood field that affects everyone nearby) and [Bard](/itemlist/guide/classes/#bard) (colony-wide mood and inspiration upkeep, also doubles as the colony's trade/negotiation "face") are the dedicated picks. [Commander](/itemlist/guide/classes/#commander)'s aura half (Provisioner, Taskmaster, Commander auras) does similar work passively, outside of combat.
[↑ Back to top](#top)

#### General Workforce
This is "buy a helper instead of upgrading a pawn." [Summoner](/itemlist/guide/classes/#summoner)'s Summon Minion is explicitly built for basic labor. [Golemancer](/itemlist/guide/classes/#golemancer)'s golems haul and clean once built. [Necromancer](/itemlist/guide/classes/#necromancer)'s undead can be set to growing, construction, crafting, or general labor. All three Pet Class picks double as workforce here.
[↑ Back to top](#top)

## Combat jobs

#### Hunter
Covers single-target hunters and assassins. [Ranger](/itemlist/guide/classes/#ranger) (animal companion, generalist), [Sniper](/itemlist/guide/classes/#sniper) with its own tier ladder (Sniper → Master Sniper → Legendary Sniper), and [Psionic](/itemlist/guide/classes/#psionic)'s physical-augmented strikes. Ranger specifically wants a bow in hand — Arrow Storm doesn't work without one. [Shadow](/itemlist/guide/classes/#shadow) is the pure assassin of the bunch — teleport behind a target, land one heavy crit-capable hit, then get pulled back out before anything can retaliate.
[↑ Back to top](#top)

#### Tank
[Deathknight](/itemlist/guide/classes/#deathknight) is the purpose-built answer: its Hate resource builds from taking and dealing damage and directly increases armor, and it's explicitly strongest paired with a Thrumbo Axe. [Gladiator](/itemlist/guide/classes/#gladiator) and [Paladin](/itemlist/guide/classes/#paladin) both hold a front line too, just without Deathknight's damage-fed defense. [Monk](/itemlist/guide/classes/#monk) tanks by avoiding hits rather than soaking them — high melee dodge and hit chance, unarmed combo strikes, and an AoE burst that can interrupt an enemy's ability mid-cast — but it's a dodge tank, not a damage sponge, and a bad dodge roll against something heavy can end it fast.
[↑ Back to top](#top)

#### AoE Blaster
[Fire Mage](/itemlist/guide/classes/#fire-mage) hits hardest, but it's explicitly "unpredictable and difficult to hit with," and its fire spreads — including to your own base. [Lightning Mage](/itemlist/guide/classes/#lightning-mage) trades some of that raw damage for a wider, more random spread. [Brightmage](/itemlist/guide/classes/#brightmage) is the side pick of the three: Light Lance is a rapid 12-shot burst and Sunfire is its single biggest hit, both fired from range with no friendly-fire risk, plus a blinding Light Burst for crowd control on the side. [Super Soldier](/itemlist/guide/classes/#super-soldier) is the martial version — a grenade launcher, a deployable mortar, and a breaching charge that does 30x damage to structures, no mana or stamina-pool micromanagement required.
[↑ Back to top](#top)

#### Crowd Control
Also covers area denial. [Ice Mage](/itemlist/guide/classes/#ice-mage) doesn't hit hard, it hits often — slows, freezes, and bleeds, and is described as best paired with a real damage-dealer doing the actual killing. [Warlock/Succubus](/itemlist/guide/classes/#warlock) (a gendered pair) don't hit hard either — Dominate spreads fear and mental breaks (or saps physical prowess, gender-dependent), Attraction/Repulsion shove crowds around, and none of it is lethal on its own. [Shaman](/itemlist/guide/classes/#shaman)'s totems (lightning, healing, earthquake) plus its guardian-spirit companions cover this too.
[↑ Back to top](#top)

#### Combat Support
Also covers healing. [Priest](/itemlist/guide/classes/#priest) is dedicated and literally incapable of violence: Heal, Purify, Healing Circle, and Resurrection. [Shaman](/itemlist/guide/classes/#shaman)'s healing totem doubles here. [Commander](/itemlist/guide/classes/#commander)'s commands half (Stay Alert!, Move Out!, Hold the Line!) buffs a squad mid-fight rather than healing it. [Enchanter](/itemlist/guide/classes/#enchanter)'s combat kit (Enchant Weapon, Polymorph, Blank Mind) is support-flavored utility rather than pure healing.
[↑ Back to top](#top)

#### Summoner {#summoner-job}
Also covers fodder. [Summoner](/itemlist/guide/classes/#summoner) is the lowest-investment option (Summon Minion, Pylon, Mana Mine, Elemental, no upfront crafting needed). [Necromancer](/itemlist/guide/classes/#necromancer) raises an army from corpses instead of fighting personally. [Golemancer](/itemlist/guide/classes/#golemancer) needs its golems built at a workbench first, which is more setup, but produces sturdier, more controllable companions than either.
[↑ Back to top](#top)

#### Battlefield Control
Also covers general utility. [Arcane Mage](/itemlist/guide/classes/#arcane-mage) repositions the fight (teleport, blink, forced summon) more than it damages anything directly. [Chronomancer](/itemlist/guide/classes/#chronomancer)'s Accelerate Time curses everyone in an area with rapid aging rather than a direct kill, and Recall doubles as pseudo self-healing mid-fight — snap back to a marked state from moments ago and undo whatever just happened to you — on top of its non-combat repair/cure utility. [Enchanter](/itemlist/guide/classes/#enchanter)'s Polymorph turns enemies into animals mid-fight. [Faceless](/itemlist/guide/classes/#faceless) can Mimic any ability on the battlefield, friend or foe.
[↑ Back to top](#top)

#### Glass Cannon
[Blood Mage](/itemlist/guide/classes/#blood-mage) doesn't use mana for its own spells — every spell after the first is paid for out of a self-generated Blood Magic pool, fueled by its own bleeding or an ally's. [Chaos Mage](/itemlist/guide/classes/#chaos-mage) gets a random spell pool instead of choosing one, at a flat mana-cost penalty across every spell it casts. Both are high-ceiling, no-safety-net picks — not the move for a viewer's first pawn.
[↑ Back to top](#top)
